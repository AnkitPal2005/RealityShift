# Scriptural Sources & Verification Guide

This guide establishes the primary repositories, canonical recensions, citation rules, and evidence audit standards for researching Sanatan Dharma narratives in the **Sanatan Katha** pipeline.

---

## 1. Strict Source-Verification Standards

### Zero-Guessing Rule
1. **Never Assume or Invent**: Never invent, estimate, or hallucinate chapter numbers, verse numbers, Sanskrit shlokas, English/Hindi translations, publication years, or historical dates.
2. **Explicit Edition Stating**: Chapter numbering frequently differs between recensions and publishers (e.g., Gita Press vs. Motilal Banarsidass vs. BORI Critical Edition vs. Venkateshwara Steam Press). **Always specify the exact edition and recension used.**
3. **Traceable Access**: If a primary source was not directly opened and read (whether in print or via a verified digital repository such as Wisdom Library, Gita Press e-books, SARIT, or Muktabodha), you **must not** label it as `[VERIFIED]`.

---

## 2. Standardized Evidence Labels

Every factual claim in a research report must carry one of three explicit labels:

| Label | Meaning | Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `[VERIFIED]` | Primary text directly inspected | The researcher has directly verified the statement in a specified primary Sanskrit or traditional text (exact edition, canto/parva, chapter, and verse). |
| `[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]` | Supported by secondary scholarly source | The claim is documented in reputable scholarly commentaries, academic books (e.g., MLBD AITM series), or gazetteers, but the primary Sanskrit manuscript lines were not directly inspected. |
| `[UNVERIFIED]` | Insufficient evidence / Folk tradition | The claim is widely repeated in popular media, TV serials, or oral tradition, but lacks direct primary scriptural citation. Must be clearly labeled as such before use in storytelling. |

---

## 3. Distinguishing Scripture from Interpretation & Folklore

A senior researcher must keep three layers distinct:

1. **Direct Scriptural Statement**: What the primary text literally says (e.g., *Skanda Purana states that Barbarika was the Yaksha Suryavarcas cursed by Brahma to be beheaded by Hari*).
2. **Researcher Interpretation & Deductions**: Logical deductions or philosophical explanations that connect narrative dots (e.g., *Barbarika's vow to fight for the losing side created an inescapable mathematical paradox that would wipe out both armies*). This is an analytical interpretation of why Krishna acted, not a verbatim verse.
3. **Folk Tradition / Sthala Lore (*Lok Manyata*)**: Oral stories developed around sacred places (e.g., *Krishna disguising as a Brahmin to test Barbarika by piercing Peepal leaves, and the unearthing of his head at Khatu, Sikar*). This must be clearly introduced as *"Lok manyata ke anusaar..."* and never masqueraded as Sanskrit scripture.

## 4. Primary Canonical Corpora & Edition Varieties

### 4.1 Itihasa (Epic History)

#### Valmiki Ramayana
- **Structure**: 6 Kandas (Bala, Ayodhya, Aranya, Kishkindha, Sundara, Yuddha) + Uttara Kanda.
- **Editions**:
  - *Gita Press Gorakhpur*: 2 Volumes with Hindi commentary (Traditional Northern Vulgate).
  - *Baroda Critical Edition*: Oriental Institute, MS University of Baroda (7 volumes, 1960–1975). Note: Chapter and verse numbering differ significantly between Gita Press and the Baroda Critical Edition. Always specify which is cited.
- **Major Regional Adaptations**:
  - *Ramcharitmanas* (Goswami Tulsidas, Awadhi, 16th century CE).
  - *Kamba Ramayanam* / *Ramavataram* (Kambar, Tamil, 12th century CE).
  - *Krittivasi Ramayan* (Krittibas Ojha, Bengali, 15th century CE).
  - *Adhyatma Ramayana* (Brahmanda Purana extract).

#### Vyasa Mahabharata
- **Structure**: 18 Parvas + *Harivamsha Parva*.
- **Editions**:
  - *BORI Critical Edition* (Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute, Pune, 1919–1966, 19 volumes). Represents the pruned critical text established from hundreds of regional manuscripts.
  - *Gita Press Gorakhpur* (6 Volumes, Sanskrit-Hindi, based on the Northern Vulgate / Nilakantha commentary). Contains many episodes omitted from the BORI Critical text.
- **Chapter Numbering Variance**: Sarga/Adhyaya numbering in Gita Press frequently differs by several chapters from BORI. Cite both if possible, or name the edition explicitly.

---

### 4.2 The Eighteen Mahapuranas
1. **Srimad Bhagavata Purana**: 12 Skandhas, ~18,000 verses. Premier text for Krishna Leela, Dashavatara, and Bhakti philosophy.
2. **Vishnu Purana**: 6 Amsas. Foundational cosmogony and genealogies.
3. **Shiva Purana**: 7 Samhitas (Vidyeshwara, Rudra, Sata-rudra, Koti-rudra, Uma, Kailasha, Vayu). Key source for Jyotirlingas, Ardhanarishvara, and Parvati-Shiva leelas.
4. **Skanda Purana**: The largest Purana (~81,000 verses across 7 Khandas: Maheshwara, Vaishnava, Brahma, Kashi, Avanti, Nagara, Prabhasa).
   - *Important Note on Recensions*: The Venkateshwara Steam Press edition (translated in the MLBD AITM series by Dr. G.V. Tagare) differs in chapter divisions from local Bengali or Southern palm-leaf recensions.
   - Primary text for *Barbarik / Guptakshetra* (Maheshwara Khanda, Kaumarika Khanda), *Jagannath Puri* (Vaishnava Khanda, Utkala Khanda), and *Kashi Mahatmya*.
5. **Markandeya Purana**: Contains the 700-verse *Devi Mahatmya* (Chapters 81–93).
6. **Padma Purana, Brahmavaivarta, Agni, Garuda, Linga, Kurma, Matsya, Varaha, Vamana, Bhavishya, Vayu, Brahmanda**.

## 5. Reputable Repositories & Search Portals

When accessing texts to verify claims, use verified repositories:
1. **Wisdom Library (wisdomlib.org)**: Houses complete, unabridged English translations of major Puranas (MLBD AITM series, trans. G.V. Tagare, J.L. Shastri, etc.), Valmiki Ramayana, and Mahabharata.
2. **Gita Press Gorakhpur (gitapress.org / archive.org)**: Complete Sanskrit originals with word-for-word and running Hindi translations.
3. **SARIT (Search and Retrieval of Indic Texts - sarit.indology.info)**: Scholarly TEI-encoded Sanskrit texts with philological apparatus.
4. **Muktabodha Indological Research Institute (muktabodha.org)**: Digitized palm-leaf manuscripts and Tantric/Shaiva literature.
5. **Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute (BORI Online)**: Critical Edition apparatus and digital scans.

## 6. Disentangling Pop-Culture Misconceptions

| Modern Pop-Culture Claim | Scriptural Reality / Direct Source Finding | Evidence Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Draupadi laughed and mocked Duryodhana saying *"Andhe ka beta andha"* | In the *Sabha Parva* of Vyasa Mahabharata (both BORI Critical Edition and Gita Press), Draupadi is **not present** when Duryodhana falls into the water of the Maya Sabha. The servants and the Pandava brothers laughed; Draupadi never spoke that dialogue. | `[VERIFIED]` (Debunked TV Fiction) |
| Karna was cursed by Parashurama because he was a Kshatriya | Parashurama cursed him because Karna lied about his identity by pretending to be a Brahmana disciple (*Mahabharata, Shanti Parva 3.20*). Parashurama's vow was not to teach Kshatriyas, but the curse was specifically for deceit (*Mithyacara*). | `[VERIFIED]` (Scriptural Fact) |
| Barbarika pierced Peepal leaves with Krishna's foot placed on one leaf | In the Sanskrit *Skanda Purana (Kaumarika Khanda Ch. 60–66)*, Krishna beheads Barbarika directly to fulfill Brahma's curse on Yaksha Suryavarcas and avert war disruption. The Peepal leaf piercing test is a beloved **Rajasthani / North Indian folk tradition (Lok Manyata)**, not found in the Sanskrit Purana text. | `[VERIFIED]` (Folk Tradition, not Sanskrit Purana) |
| Lakshmana drew a glowing barrier (*Lakshman Rekha*) around Sita's hut | Valmiki Ramayana (Aranya Kanda) has **no mention** of the Lakshman Rekha. It first appears centuries later in regional traditions, popularized in Goswami Tulsidas's *Ramcharitmanas* (Lanka Kanda dialogue between Mandodari and Ravana) and folk plays. | `[VERIFIED]` (Regional Tradition / Lok Manyata) |
| Ravana was a moral ascetic who never touched Sita out of respect | In *Valmiki Ramayana (Yuddha Kanda 13.30–38)*, Ravana confesses to his ministers that he was cursed by Nalakubara (son of Kubera) after assaulting Rambha: if he ever forced an unwilling woman, his head would burst into seven pieces. | `[VERIFIED]` (Scriptural Fact) |
