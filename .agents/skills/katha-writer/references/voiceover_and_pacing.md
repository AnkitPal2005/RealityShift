# Voiceover Pacing, Spoken Delivery & Audio Guide

This guide details the voiceover architecture, pacing, performance notation, exact duration calculation, and sound design for **Sanatan Katha** YouTube Shorts scripts.

---

## 1. The Kathavachak Voice Architecture

The narration must sound like an oral storytelling performance by a revered, seasoned **Kathavachak**:

- **Conversational Tone**: Warm, grounded, authoritative yet deeply intimate.
- **Rhythmic Cadence**: Natural ebb and flow of tension; lowering pitch during mystery and swelling with reverence during divine revelations.
- **Audience Connection**: Engaging the listener directly with conversational markers (*"जरा सोच के देखो..."*, *"कहते हैं..."*, *"पर यहीं पर एक ऐसा मोड़ आया..."*).

## 2. Spoken Hindi vs. Stiff Translation

| Stiff / Textbook Hindi (AVOID) | Natural Kathavachak Hindi (USE) | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *महाभारत के युद्ध के प्रारम्भ होने से पूर्व भगवान श्रीकृष्ण ने बर्बरीक नामक योद्धा का मस्तक दान में प्राप्त किया था।* | *महाभारत शुरू होने से ठीक पहले... [PAUSE 0.5s] श्री कृष्ण ने अपने ही एक परम भक्त का शीश माँग लिया। सोचो... ऐसा क्यों किया?* | Creates immediate curiosity, breaks the sentence into punchy, conversational spoken units. |
| *उसके पास तीन ऐसे बाण उपस्थित थे जिनसे वह समस्त सेना का संहार करने में पूर्णतया सक्षम था।* | *उसके तरकश में सिर्फ तीन बाण थे। [EMPHASIS] और वो तीन बाण... पूरी महाभारत को एक पल में खत्म कर सकते थे!* | Punchy, dramatic, creates visual imagery instantly. |

## 3. Dialogue Quotation & Integrity Rules

1. **No Fictitious Quotes as Scripture**: Never put invented dialogue inside quotation marks (`"..."`) as if it were a literal Sanskrit shloka translation from Vyasa or the Puranas.
2. **Narration Paraphrasing**: If a deity or character speaks in creative dramatization, phrase it naturally through the storyteller's voice (*"श्री कृष्ण ने मुस्कुराते हुए समझाया कि..."* or *"बर्बरीक ने शीश झुकाकर कहा कि प्रभु..."*).
3. **Audit Trail**: Every direct dialogue or spoken exchange must be cataloged in Section G (Fact-Check Notes) as either **Literal Scriptural Translation** or **Creative Dramatization**.

## 4. Exact Word Count & Timing Formula

Do not estimate durations vaguely. Calculate using the tested empirical formula for Kathavachak delivery:

- **Kathavachak Spoken Pace**: **2.2 to 2.4 Hindi words per second** (accounting for breathing beats).
- **Pause Summation**: Add the exact sum of all marked pauses:
  - `[PAUSE 0.5s]` = +0.5s
  - `[PAUSE 1.0s]` = +1.0s
- **Formula**:
  $$\text{Duration (s)} = \left(\frac{\text{Word Count}}{2.3}\right) + \sum \text{Pauses (s)}$$

### Reference Duration Table:
| Target Duration | Optimal Hindi Word Count | Pause Allowance | Story Arc Density |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **60 Seconds** | 125 – 135 words | 4–6 seconds of pauses | High tempo, 6 scenes. |
| **75 Seconds** | 155 – 170 words | 6–8 seconds of pauses | Standard optimal format, 7–8 scenes. |
| **90 Seconds** | 180 – 195 words | 8–10 seconds of pauses | Rich narrative, 8–9 scenes. |

## 5. Sound Design & Audio Cues

1. **Ambient Music Bed**: Cinematic devotional ambient pad (80–88 BPM, volume strictly balanced at `0.10–0.12` beneath voiceover).
2. **Zero Conch Spam**: Never insert the loud conch horn (*shankh*) repeatedly across shorts. Use subtle temple bell chimes, warm tanpura resonance, bansuri swells, or low atmospheric strings.
